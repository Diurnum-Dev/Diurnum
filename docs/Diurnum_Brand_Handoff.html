The name

Diurnum is a Latin noun meaning account-book, day-book — attested in Juvenal, used in Rome for the running record of daily transactions. Late Latin diurnum became Old French journal; the sense “a daily record of transactions” is first recorded in 1565.

The journal entry — the atom of double-entry bookkeeping — is, etymologically, a diurnum entry. The name is not a metaphor for the product. It is the product, in its oldest form.

On explaining the name

Yes, it must be explained. We treat that as a feature — the explanation is a small, satisfying story that the right user remembers and repeats. A name you grow into wears longer than one you exhaust in a week. This audience reads; they like a name with a reason behind it. The in-group recognition (“oh — journal, of course”) is not a tax. It is the point.

Positioning
A record you own, can read, and can prove — for as long as you keep it.

For the technical founder-operator who keeps their own books and wants a ledger that will outlive any vendor, Diurnum is a desktop accounting workspace that records everything as portable, double-entry Beancount text.

Unlike privacy-first budgeting apps — which share the values but stop at expense tracking — Diurnum keeps the actual books: double-entry, with real financial statements, valid in the Beancount ecosystem. And unlike cloud accounting SaaS, nothing is hidden and nothing is captive.

The category is neither budgeting nor accounting SaaS. It is the day-book, restored: auditable, version-controlled, double-entry accounting you own outright.

Key positioning shift

Local-first, privacy, and “you own your files” are now table stakes — the whole category claims them. Lead instead on: double-entry depth, the Beancount standard (not just a readable file), the operator audience, git-native IDE feel, and auditability. These are the claims no competitor can cheaply copy.

Semantic assignment — do not deviate

Lapis = primary buttons, active nav, “approve,” diff additions, “+ New” badges. Oxblood = destructive actions, validation errors, diff deletions, “Duplicate” warnings. Gilt is not a semantic state — reserve for the rarest of highlights. Verify every text/background pair at WCAG AA before shipping.

The visual thesis is the contrast: serif chrome over a monospace ledger — an ancient practice, a modern tool. The serif appears in headings, navigation labels, the wordmark, and marketing copy. Monospace appears exclusively on ledger text, diff surfaces, amounts, metadata, and code. UI chrome uses Inter for legibility at small sizes.

Diff treatment — a core brand surface

The diff is the product’s soul. Show one on every marketing surface — landing page, README, social card. A real before/after entry says more than any paragraph about transparency and auditability.

Voice
We say “Here is the entry, and the day it was written. Approve it, and the record stands.”
We say “Your books are Beancount text. Open them in Fava. Leave any time.”
We say “AI proposes. You approve. The day-book records the result.”
Never “Supercharge your finances with AI-powered bookkeeping! 🚀”
Never “Revolutionize your financial future with cutting-edge automation.”

Personality: the learned, composed craftsperson. Precise, literate, calm. Short, measured sentences with a sense of weight and permanence.

Guardrail: We are a scriptorium, not a Renaissance fair. Classical register lives in proportion, ruled lines, ink, and the serif — not in ornament. One well-placed archival note does the work; never costumed or fussy.

What Diurnum is not
  • ✕Not a budgeting app. It keeps the books; it does not draw spending bars. Different job, different buyer.
  • ✕Not AI-first. The durable value is a trustworthy, local-first, own-it-forever ledger. AI is a capability, not the marque. Never lead with “AI.”
  • ✕Not a costume. The classical register is restraint and permanence, not pastiche. If a flourish doesn’t earn its place, cut it.
  • ✕Not a black box. If a feature can’t show its work — and the day it did the work — it doesn’t ship.
