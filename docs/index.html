What you are looking at

Diurnum is accounting software for solo businesses, consultants, and anyone else who does their own bookkeeping. It is not a budgeting app, a cloud bookkeeping service, or a nicer skin on QuickBooks. It is a local workspace for maintaining a clean, readable ledger.

It keeps real books

Diurnum uses double-entry accounting, so your books can produce an income statement, expense breakdown, account balances, and a balance sheet. Books, not budget bars.

Your ledger is a folder

The source of truth is plain Beancount text on your Mac. Open it in Diurnum, Fava, Git, or a text editor. If Diurnum vanished, your books would still be readable.

Imports are reviewable

Bring in CSV statements from your bank, review Diurnum’s suggested entries, then approve the exact ledger changes before they are written.

AI never gets the pen

Optional AI can suggest categories or complete entries from your ledger context, but approval stays with you. No autonomous bookkeeping. No silent rewrites.

Accounting software should not own your accounting.

QuickBooks is the enemy because it taught small businesses to accept slow software, opaque data, and a monthly bill just to read their own books. Diurnum takes the opposite side: the ledger belongs to the business.

The goal is not to clone QuickBooks with friendlier colors. The goal is to make the smallest useful desktop app around a durable accounting record you can inspect, version, validate, and carry away.

Instead of SaaS

A local Workspace folder with your ledger, documents, imports, settings, and history.

Instead of lock-in

Standard Beancount files that open in other plain-text accounting tools.

Instead of magic

Reviewable diffs, source rows, validation, and approval before anything becomes trusted state.

How Diurnum works
1
Create a local Workspace

Diurnum creates a folder for one business: readable ledger files, local app state, imported documents, snapshots, and optional Git history.

2
Import bank CSVs

Download statements from your bank and bring them in. Diurnum normalizes rows, detects duplicates, and keeps source files with the Workspace.

3
Review suggested entries

Rules, history, and optional AI can suggest payees, accounts, and transfer matches. You see the Beancount entry before approval.

4
Approve readable ledger changes

Approval writes balanced plain-text entries, validates the ledger, and keeps the record portable. Reports come from those files.

Review before writing

You approve ledger changes as text

Diurnum can help assemble the entry, but the final record is still a readable Beancount change you can inspect before it becomes part of your books.

Diurnum (dy-UR-num) is a Latin noun meaning account-book, day-book. Late Latin diurnum became Old French journal, the daily record of transactions.

The name is not a metaphor for the product. A journal entry, the atom of double-entry bookkeeping, is a diurnum entry: a day's transaction written into a book that endures.
