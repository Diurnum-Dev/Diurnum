Design System · v2

A record that endures.

Diurnum is ink on vellum — the scholar's day-book, not the accountant's office. Serif chrome over a monospace ledger: an ancient practice, a modern tool. The classical register lives in proportion, ruled lines, and the serif — never in ornament.

Philosophy

Three rules that override every other decision. They carry over from the old system in spirit — the page, the hairline, the room to breathe — but the register is now classical and composed.

01
The printed page, not the floating card.
Separate surfaces with ruled hairlines and generous margins. Elevation is a whisper reserved for genuinely floating things — dialogs, popovers, the palette.
02
Ink and weight over hue.
Most emphasis comes from the serif, from weight, from a faint wash of lapis — not from color. Reach for the palette sparingly and never for decoration.
03
Restraint is the flourish.
A scriptorium, not a Renaissance fair. If an ornament doesn't earn its place, cut it. Permanence reads as calm, unhurried composition.

Radii & elevation

One border width: 1px. Radii are modest — the printed page, not the pill. Elevation is nearly gone: warm, soft, and reserved for things that genuinely float. Hairline borders do the separating everywhere else.
