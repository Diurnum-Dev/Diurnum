# Diurnum — Brand Brief

## One line

**Diurnum** is a local-first, double-entry accounting workspace built on plain text. The day-book, restored: every entry written down as it happens, in a record you own, can read, and can prove.

## Essence

The oldest idea in accounting, made new. *Diurnum* is the Roman word for the day-book — and the direct ancestor of the word *journal*. A ledger you keep the way books were always meant to be kept: in your own hand, in plain sight, traceable to the day each line was written.

---

## The name (this one needs telling — that's the point)

*Diurnum* (dy-UR-num) is a Latin noun meaning **account-book, day-book** — attested in Juvenal, used in Rome for the running record of daily transactions. Late Latin *diurnum* became Old French *journal*; the sense "a daily record of transactions" enters the books in 1565. The journal entry — the atom of double-entry bookkeeping — is, etymologically, a *diurnum* entry.

So the name is not a metaphor for the product. It *is* the product, in its oldest form: the daily book of account.

Yes, it must be explained. We treat that as a feature, not a bug — the explanation is a small, satisfying story that the right user remembers and repeats. A name you grow into wears longer than a name you exhaust in a week.

---

## Temperature — and how it differs from the alternatives

Three different souls were on the table. This brief commits to the third:

- **The stone direction** (cool, mineral, sage-green, accounting-house): solid but expected — quietly *fintech*.
- **The workshop direction** (warm, handmade, patch-and-quilt): friendly and legible, the well-kept bench.
- **Diurnum — the scriptorium direction:** *ink on vellum.* Classical, composed, unhurried. Not the accountant's office and not the maker's bench — the **scholar's desk.** Erudite without being cold; old-world without being antique. The calm of a thing built to outlast its software.

Where the others say "trustworthy tool," Diurnum says "**a record that endures.**"

## Positioning statement

For the technical founder-operator who keeps their own books and wants a ledger that will outlive any vendor, **Diurnum** is a desktop accounting workspace that records everything as portable, double-entry Beancount text — a day-book you write, own, and can read a decade from now.

Unlike privacy-first **budgeting apps**, which share the values but stop at expense tracking, Diurnum keeps the actual books: double-entry, with real financial statements, valid in the Beancount ecosystem. And unlike **cloud accounting SaaS**, nothing is hidden and nothing is captive — every figure traces to the entry and the day that produced it.

The category is neither budgeting nor accounting SaaS. It is **the day-book, restored**: auditable, version-controlled, double-entry accounting you own outright.

---

## Who it's for

**The Founder-Operator.** A technically literate solo business owner who keeps their own business books — a developer or indie founder on macOS who lives in the terminal, version-controls everything, and resents software that won't show its work. They read; they like a name with a reason behind it; they want a P&L they can trust and a ledger they can walk away with, intact, at any time.

This audience is exactly the one for whom an erudite name *adds* trust rather than friction: the in-group recognition ("oh — *journal*, of course") is a feature, not a tax.

**Not for (yet):** consumer budgeters, multi-client bookkeepers, payroll-heavy firms, ecommerce, multi-entity operations, or non-technical users.

## Brand promise

> A record you own, can read, and can prove — for as long as you keep it.

- Your books live as open Beancount text on your machine, valid in tools you don't control.
- Every entry traces to its source and its day; every change is visible and reversible.
- AI proposes; you approve; the day-book records the result.

## Messaging pillars

1. **The day-book, restored.** Double-entry accounting in its oldest and most durable form — real income statement and balance sheet, not spending categories. Books, not budgets.
2. **Written in your own hand.** Plain Beancount text you own outright. Open in Fava, validate with `bean-check`, leave for any plain-text tool. Ownership that's portable, not just readable.
3. **Every line, accountable.** Each figure traces to the entry — and the day — that produced it. AI suggestions are grounded in your ledger and cite their sources. Numbers are computed, never invented.
4. **Built to endure.** Local-first, private, version-controlled. A record designed to outlast the software that wrote it.

## Voice

**Personality:** the learned, composed craftsperson — a scholar who keeps immaculate books. Precise, literate, calm. Speaks plainly but with care for the words; never breezy, never hype, never cold. Confident enough to be brief.

**Voice principles:**
- Plainspoken, but with a sense of weight and permanence. Short, measured sentences.
- Lets one well-placed classical or archival note do the work; never costumed or fussy. (We are a scriptorium, not a Renaissance fair.)
- Explains when explaining rewards the reader — the name's story, the etymology of *journal* — and trusts them with the rest.
- Honest about tradeoffs. Respects a discerning reader who values control and permanence.

**We sound like:** "Here is the entry, and the day it was written. Approve it, and the record stands."
**We never sound like:** "Supercharge your finances with AI! 🚀"

## Visual direction

Move decisively off the sage/accounting-house palette. The world here is **ink, vellum, and inscription** — warm and archival rather than cool and mineral.

- **Palette:**
  - *Ground:* warm parchment / vellum off-white (e.g. `#F7F1E3`–`#FBF6EC` range), not clinical white.
  - *Ink:* a deep iron-gall near-black with a brown undertone (e.g. `#1C1A17`), softer than pure black.
  - *Primary accent:* a single classical hue — **oxblood / Roman red** (`#7B2D26`) **or lapis / ink-blue** (`#243B6B`). Pick one as the signature; it replaces sage entirely. Oxblood reads warmer and more distinctive; lapis reads more scholarly and calm.
  - *Secondary:* muted ochre / gold (`#B68A3E`) for rare highlights — the gilt-initial accent, used sparingly.
  - *Diff / validation:* keep semantics, but tuned to the palette — a soft ink-blue for additions, an oxblood for deletions/errors, rather than the green/red default. (Verify accessibility contrast.)
  - Light/parchment mode first; the palette has an obvious candlelit dark variant (ink ground, parchment text) for later.
- **Type:**
  - *Brand & display:* a humanist or old-style **serif** with classical proportion and a whisper of inscriptional Roman capital — think the dignity of a chapter opening, not a law firm logo. This is the biggest single departure from the workshop/stone directions, which were sans + mono.
  - *Prose / UI:* a quiet humanist sans for legibility.
  - *Ledger & diff surfaces:* monospace as ever (JetBrains Mono / SF Mono) — the one place the modern engine shows through the old-world skin. The contrast (serif chrome, monospace ledger) is the visual thesis: an ancient practice, a modern tool.
- **Logo / mark direction:** explore an **illuminated initial "D"**, a closed codex/day-book spine, or a single ruled ledger-line under a Roman-capital wordmark. Restrained, inked, precise. No gloss, no gradient; the feel of letterpress on heavy stock.
- **Texture & feel:** the scriptorium desk. Generous margins like a well-set page; ruled baselines; the calm of a manuscript. Matte, warm, permanent.

## What Diurnum is not

- **Not a budgeting app.** It keeps the books; it does not draw spending bars.
- **Not an AI-first brand.** The durable value is a trustworthy, local-first, own-it-forever ledger. AI is a capability, not the marque.
- **Not a costume.** The classical register is restraint and permanence, not pastiche. If a flourish doesn't earn its place, it goes.
- **Not a black box.** If a feature can't show its work — and the day it did the work — it doesn't ship.

## Naming & identity housekeeping

- **Spoken/written brand:** Diurnum (dy-UR-num). Pronunciation guide belongs in early brand materials and the site footer.
- **Availability (strong):** npm, PyPI, and the bare GitHub handle `diurnum` are all open; no accounting/finance product uses the name. The only live use of the exact word is an Episcopal prayer app (out of class, non-competing).
- **Domains:** confirm `diurnum.com`; fallback `getdiurnum.com` (matches the open GitHub handle) or `diurnum.app`. Note the phone/radio test is this name's weak point — test the domain aloud with real listeners before committing.
- **Before any spend:** the say-and-spell test with 5+ people (watch for stumbles on stress and spelling), then formal USPTO clearance in Class 9 (downloadable software) and Class 42 (SaaS). Lock domain + handles the week the mark clears.
- **Known tradeoff to accept going in:** the name must be explained and is not phonetically obvious. This brief's entire bet is that, for this audience, the explanation is an asset and the erudition builds loyalty. If that bet feels wrong for the product you want, Patchbook is the legible alternative.
